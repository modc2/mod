These four systems need no trusted setup, so they are cheap enough to prove right here. The secret never leaves the request, and the proof is checked by two independent verifiers before you see it. The snark systems (groth16, plonk, fflonk) need a proving key; bring one to the API.

Schnorr

zero-knowledge

“I hold this key.” Fiat-Shamir, context-bound, cannot be replayed elsewhere.

Merkle

not zero-knowledge

“This leaf is in that set.” An inclusion path, verified but not hidden — labelled not zero-knowledge.

DLEQ

zero-knowledge

One discrete log, two bases: proves A = xG and B = xH share an x without saying what x is. H is derived by hashing, so nobody knows its log.

Pedersen

not zero-knowledge

C = vG + rH, opened. Binding, and hiding until you open it — but opening reveals v, so this one is labelled not zero-knowledge everywhere it appears.

Pay for a proof that does not exist yet. The reward sits in escrow from the moment you post, and the spec is mechanical — a verification key plus constraints on the public signals — so whether a submission qualifies is decided by arithmetic and not by argument.

Five verifiers, written by five sets of people, three of which are not on this box at all. Agreement between them is the product; disagreement is the thing worth seeing.
